# PulseDeck — Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** 30 September 2026
**Publisher:** Fokion Papanikolaou

PulseDeck ("the app") is a desktop system-monitor for Windows 10/11. This
policy explains, in plain language, exactly what data the app sends, where it
goes, and what we do with it.

> **TL;DR — we don't collect anything.** No accounts, no analytics, no
> advertising, no telemetry. Everything the app records stays on your PC. The
> network is used only for the optional weather widget, an optional update
> check, and features you start yourself (showing your public IP, the DNS
> benchmark, a network latency check and the speed test) — each described
> below.

---

## 1. What we collect
**Nothing.** PulseDeck does not have any servers, accounts, sign-in, telemetry,
crash reporting, advertising or analytics. We never see how you use the app.

## 2. What the app stores on your PC
All of these live in one folder in your user profile (the package's local
data folder, `%APPDATA%\PulseDeck` for the installer build, or next to the
executable for the portable build). **None of them is ever uploaded.**

- `config.json` — your preferences (metrics, theme, language, position,
  weather city, data limit, the last five speed-test results, …).
- `history.csv` — one line per minute of CPU, RAM, GPU and network load, kept
  for 7 days, for the History tab.
- `netusage.json` — upload/download totals per day, for the data-usage counter.
- `culprits.json` — during a CPU or RAM spike, the **name** of the process
  that was using the most (for example `chrome.exe`), so the History tab can
  show what caused it. Only process names and a percentage or size are kept —
  no window titles, file names or command lines — for 7 days.
- `pulsedeck.log` — a diagnostics log that is written only when something goes
  wrong (an error, or a background task stopping), capped at 256 KB. You can
  open it from *Settings → About* and choose to attach it to a bug report; the
  app never sends it anywhere by itself.

Deleting the folder, or uninstalling the app, removes all of it.

## 3. What goes over the network
- **Weather (optional widget).** If the weather cell is enabled and no city is
  set, the app first asks the **Windows Location Service** for your position —
  this happens entirely on your PC (nothing is sent anywhere to get the fix)
  and only works if you have allowed Location in Windows Settings. Depending on
  what is available, the app then makes HTTPS requests to:
  - **bigdatacloud.net** — only when the Windows location fix succeeded: the
    coordinates are sent to their free reverse-geocoding endpoint solely to
    display your city's name. → https://www.bigdatacloud.com/privacy-and-cookie-policy
  - **ipapi.co** — only when Windows Location is unavailable: an approximate
    location (city / latitude / longitude) is derived from your **public IP**.
    → https://ipapi.co/privacy/
  - **open-meteo.com** (weather + geocoding) — to resolve a typed city name to
    coordinates and fetch the current conditions and short forecast. Open-Meteo
    requires no API key and does not track users.
    → https://open-meteo.com/en/terms

  If you set a city manually, no location lookup of any kind happens; if you
  turn the weather cell off, none of these calls are made.

- **Public IP display (System tab).** When you open *Settings → System*, the
  app asks **api.ipify.org** for your own public IP address so it can be shown
  in the Network section. The service simply echoes the address back; nothing
  else is sent, and the result is only displayed on your screen — never stored
  or transmitted elsewhere. The result is cached for 30 minutes.
  → Their privacy policy: https://www.ipify.org

- **Network latency (hover tooltip).** When you point at the Network cell, the
  app pings **1.1.1.1** (Cloudflare's public DNS address) once to show your
  current latency. It runs only while that tooltip is open, at most once every
  10 seconds; nothing but the ping itself is sent.

- **DNS Boost (Tools → Network, only when you run it).** Measures how fast 15
  well-known public DNS resolvers (Cloudflare, Google, Quad9, OpenDNS, AdGuard
  and others) answer, by sending each of them ordinary DNS lookups. Only the
  lookups themselves are sent.

- **Speed test (Tools → Network, only when you press Start).** Downloads and
  uploads test data to and from **speed.cloudflare.com** to measure your
  connection. The upload is filler bytes generated by the app, never your
  data. A test uses up to about 290 MB, which counts toward a metered plan.
  Results are kept locally only.
  → https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/

No other feature makes network requests, apart from the update check below.
(The earthquake alerts that existed in older versions were removed in 2.8.5.)

## 4. Update check (optional, on by default)
On launch, PulseDeck may contact the public **GitHub Releases API**
(`api.github.com`) to check whether a newer version is available. Only the
latest release tag is read — no personal information is sent and the request
is throttled to once every 12 hours. You can disable this from the tray menu
(*Check for updates*).

## 5. What we do **not** do
- We do **not** create accounts or ask you to sign in.
- We do **not** read your files, browser history, keystrokes, screenshots or
  network traffic.
- We do **not** send crash reports or analytics.
- We do **not** show ads.
- We do **not** sell, share or transfer any data to third parties.

## 6. Children
PulseDeck is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. We do
not knowingly collect any data from anyone.

## 7. Your rights
Because we do not collect or store personal data on our side, there is nothing
for us to access, export or delete on your behalf. You can remove everything the app
stored at any time by uninstalling it (Settings → Apps → PulseDeck →
Uninstall), or by deleting the folder described in section 2.

If this policy changes, the new version will be published in the project
